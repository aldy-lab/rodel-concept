01 — The same process from 1824

Caught in the morning.
In the tin the same day.

Nine fishing families of Concarneau bring in the catch with the traditional bolinche, from the Bay of Concarneau, the Glénan archipelago and Cape Trévignon. Every sardine is cleaned by hand, fried for thirty seconds to seal it, and laid in the tin. Never frozen.

02 — Millésime

The finest catch
of a given year.

The House of Rödel is distinguished by its selection of sardines. Millésime is a designation reserved for the finest catch of a given year, harvested at optimal maturity for exceptional richness and remarkable depth of flavour. Rödel marks on each lid when, by whom and where the fish were caught.

04 — Since 1824

The oldest cannery brand
in the world.

In 1820 the French Admiralty asked Nicolas Appert, the inventor of preserving, to build a cannery in Bordeaux. He entrusted it to his most accomplished disciple, Charles Rödel.

Rödel · a film in nine tableaux

Bretagne

  1. Tableau I

    The sea

    Everything begins on the water: the boats of Concarneau, the Glénan, Cape Trévignon, and the sardine at its best from May to October.

  2. Tableau II

    The invention that changed everything

    Napoleon offered a reward to whoever could preserve food for his armies. Nicolas Appert answered, and opened the first cannery in Massy.

  3. Tableau III

    Charles Désiré Rödel

    Appert’s most accomplished disciple builds the cannery in Bordeaux: Rödel & Fils Frères is founded, today the oldest cannery brand in the world.

  4. Tableau IV

    Cleaned by hand

    Every sardine is cleaned by hand and laid in the tin, as the women of the canneries did on the racks by the harbour.

  5. Tableau V

    Seventeen factories

    From Arcachon to Concarneau, one of France’s foremost houses of preserved foods. The beach below the Concarneau factory still bears its name.

  6. Tableau VI

    The first global brand

    Carried by the captains of merchant ships, Rödel tins reached foreign ports from Shanghai to Venezuela.

  7. Tableau VII

    A true luxury

    Ten million tins of sardines a year in France. Canned sardines were a true luxury, reserved for the most discerning tables.

  8. Tableau VIII

    The same day

    Caught in the morning, in the tin the same day, never frozen. The same process since 1824.

  9. Tableau IX

    Millésime 2025

    The finest catch of the year, found in the finest houses of Paris: a name whispered among gourmets.
